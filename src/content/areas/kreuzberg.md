Kreuzberg has a wider mix of building types than almost anywhere else I work, and the differences are big enough that two flats a hundred metres apart can throw up completely different problems.

## The building stock

There's the Gründerzeit Altbau, concentrated around Bergmannkiez and the streets off Oranienstraße — solid brick, high ceilings, Stuck where it's survived, boards on joists with a fill.

There are the 1980s IBA buildings, put up as part of a deliberate rebuilding programme. Structurally straightforward and predictable, but with their own layouts and their own ageing services.

And there are the Gewerbehöfe: courtyard commercial buildings, once workshops and light industry, now studios, offices and galleries. Some of the most interesting spaces in Berlin and some of the most awkward to fit out — unpredictable floors, walls of uncertain construction, and a power supply specified for a workshop rather than forty people with laptops.

## What I get called in for

Kreuzberg's Altbau was mostly renovated in the 1990s and early 2000s, so the dominant job here is a second-round renovation rather than a first. Bathrooms and kitchens from that era have reached the end of their service life, and stripping them out regularly shows that what's underneath — tanking, substrate prep, electrics — was done to a standard that wouldn't pass now.

Alongside that: partition work in big flats being subdivided or rearranged, studio and office fit-out in the commercial buildings, and electrical upgrades in flats where the circuits were extended rather than renewed last time round.

## Practical points

Two things shape jobs here more than the buildings do.

First is Milieuschutz. A lot of Kreuzberg sits inside Soziale Erhaltungsgebiete, and those designations restrict what counts as permissible modernisation. Layout changes, extra bathrooms and certain upgrades can need permission, and it's genuinely worth checking with the Bezirksamt while you're still planning rather than once the design is fixed.

Second is plain logistics. This is one of the densest parts of the city. Getting a van anywhere near Oranienstraße, Wrangelkiez or the Bergmannkiez is hard, parking is worse, and where a skip stands has to be sorted in advance. A courtyard often solves it, but only if the entrance is wide enough — check that before delivery day, not during.

Third is noise. Dense residential streets, plenty of people working from home, and most Hausordnungen protect a quiet spell through the middle of the day and rule out Sundays completely. I plan the loud stages around it.
