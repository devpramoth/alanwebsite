North Neukölln is one of the densest pockets of Gründerzeit housing in Berlin, and a lot of it is only now getting renovated properly for the first time. That makes the work here a bit different: less often a refresh, more often bringing a flat up to a standard it's never actually had.

## The building stock

The classic Neukölln address is a Mietshaus from around 1900, arranged as Vorderhaus, Seitenflügel and Hinterhaus around one or more courtyards. The Vorderhaus flats are usually bigger and better lit; the Hinterhaus ones smaller, darker and quieter. Same construction throughout — solid brick, lime plaster, softwood boards on joists with a fill between them, and ceilings that are generous by any modern standard.

South of the ring it changes completely. Britz, Buckow and Rudow bring 1920s estate housing, including the listed Hufeisensiedlung where what you can change is genuinely constrained, plus post-war blocks and single-family houses. Different buildings, different jobs.

## What I get called in for

The recurring Neukölln job is a flat where the bathroom got retrofitted decades ago into whatever space they could find, and hasn't been touched since. Usually a small, awkward room off the kitchen or the hall, tiled straight onto an unprepared wall with no tanking anywhere.

After that: kitchens, in flats where the electrical supply was never meant to run an oven and an induction hob at the same time. Stripping layers of woodchip off walls that then need re-skimming. And floors — Neukölln is full of original softwood boarding hiding under lino and carpet, and more often than not it's worth reclaiming rather than covering up again.

Heating leaves its mark too. Plenty of flats still carry the legacy of Ofenheizung: a tiled stove still standing, a dead flue running through a wall, or just the alcove where one used to be. Those flues limit where you can cut and what you can safely close up.

## Practical points

Access is the main one. Courtyard buildings, narrow stairwells and no lift are normal here, and that has a real effect on how long it takes to move materials and waste. Parking a van near Weserstraße or Sonnenallee is an exercise in itself, and where a skip can legally stand wants sorting before the strip-out rather than on the morning of it.

Most buildings here are tenanted rather than owner-occupied, so it's usually the Hausverwaltung whose consent matters. I'll tell you where I think you need permission, and I'll explain the work in German if that helps the conversation along.
