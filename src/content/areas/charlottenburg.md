Charlottenburg has the grandest building stock I work in, and the jobs reflect it: bigger flats, higher specifications, more original detail worth protecting, and owners who are doing up a home they mean to keep.

## The building stock

The classic Charlottenburg flat is a large pre-war apartment with tall ceilings, elaborate Stuck, original parquet, panelled doors and often Flügeltüren between reception rooms. These are bigger and better-appointed than the equivalent Altbau in the eastern districts, and the detail is more ambitious.

Mixed in with them are 1950s buildings, put up on plots cleared by wartime bombing. Genuinely different proposition — simpler construction, lower ceilings, concrete rather than timber floors a lot of the time, and none of the ornament. Often perfectly good flats, but a job in one has almost nothing in common with a job in the Altbau next door. Worth knowing which you've got before you compare prices with a neighbour.

Along Kurfürstendamm and Wilmersdorfer Straße there's a steady amount of retail and commercial work too.

## What I get called in for

Bathrooms and kitchens to a higher spec than average, in rooms where the original detail next to them has to be protected the whole way through. Parquet restoration — in Charlottenburg that's often a lot of it in one flat, and worth doing properly. Careful decorating around Stuck. And full flat renovations where the brief is to modernise the services and the wet rooms without stripping the character out of the place.

The scale changes how the job runs. A flat this size is a programme, not a single trade, and the coordination is most of the value — getting a plumber in at the right moment if the pipework is moving, keeping the tiling behind the substrate work, and protecting a parquet floor through months of boots and materials.

In the 1950s stock it's more straightforward and far more predictable: fitting out square rooms with level floors, where the constraints are the services rather than the structure.

## Practical points

Most buildings here are owner-occupied with an active WEG, and that shapes the timeline. Approval for anything structural, or anything touching the common parts, runs on the building's schedule rather than yours. Start it early.

Protection matters more here than anywhere. In a flat with original parquet, Stuck and joinery, the sheeting up, the dust containment and the route materials take through the place all need proper thought. A scratched hundred-year-old floor isn't a snagging item.

Access is easier than in the denser eastern districts — wider streets, and more buildings with a lift. If there is one, check what it'll actually take. A two-and-a-half metre board is a common thing to need and an uncommon thing to fit in.
