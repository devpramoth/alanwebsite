Prenzlauer Berg is different from the other districts I work in for one simple reason: nearly all of it has been renovated already. The Altbau here was comprehensively brought back after 1990, so the work now is rarely a first renovation and almost always a second.

## The building stock

Gründerzeit Altbau, densely built and mostly restored — Kollwitzkiez, Helmholtzkiez, the streets round Falkplatz and the Winsviertel. Ceilings are high, often three and a half metres or more. Stuck survives in a great many flats, along with original Dielen, panelled doors and sometimes double doors between the reception rooms.

Because so much of it was done at once, most of the district's bathrooms, kitchens and electrics date from roughly the same few years. They're all reaching the end of their working lives together.

## What I get called in for

The characteristic Prenzlauer Berg job is replacing a 1990s bathroom or kitchen in a flat that's otherwise in good nick. Contained, well-defined work, but it needs care, because it's happening in a finished flat with original features right next to it. Protecting Dielen and Stuck through a strip-out is a real part of the job, not an afterthought.

Second recurring job is undoing decisions from that first renovation. Original detail boxed in behind plasterboard. Cornice cut through for a cable run. Good boards buried under a floating floor laid straight over the top. A lot of that is easily reversible and makes a dramatic difference for the money.

Third is space. This is a district full of families living in flats designed for a completely different way of life, so I do a lot of partition work — dividing a big reception room to make a bedroom, building storage up into the height, and doing it in a way that respects the cornice instead of slicing through it. If you're dividing a room, think hard about light and windows before you commit.

## Practical points

Milieuschutz applies across much of Prenzlauer Berg. The Soziale Erhaltungsgebiete restrict what counts as permissible modernisation, and layout changes or extra bathrooms can need permission from the Bezirksamt. Check before you design, not after.

Ceiling height affects nearly every trade here. Painting, lighting, partition work and anything at cornice level becomes a tower job instead of a stepladder job, and that shows up in the time and the price. It isn't padding — a three-and-a-half metre wall is half as much wall again as a standard one.

Most flats here are lived in while the work happens, and a lot are owner-occupied with an active WEG. That means sequencing around people who are still living there, containing the dust properly rather than nominally, and leaving the flat habitable at the end of every day.
